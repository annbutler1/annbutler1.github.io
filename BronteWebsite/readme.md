## Design choices and iterations: 

### Home page: 

Originally I had the boxes meet each other at different heights as can be seen in the thumbnail below. I decided to rearrange the boxes and make them the same height across the rows. This made the page feel more organized and conforming. 

On a similar note, the columns were originally different widths but once I added the information to the site it became apparent that the side columns needed to be equal width to accommodate the information in a manner that did not give the impression of clutter. 

I removed many of the icons as well to help make the page more streamlined as with the information added to the page it seemed cluttered and busy which would turn the user away. I added some icons to help the user navigate more efficiently without having to read but removed any that were adding visual stress to the page.

### About Page:

After laying out all the information on the home and about page I decided that both pages were shorter in length than I expected and in order to help the user navigate the site more easily I combined the two pages. All the information and sections I originally had on the about page I combined and added to the home page.

### Information Page:

The information page wireframe has significantly more boxes than the final iteration of the website. I condensed the boxes into larger boxes with multiple pieces of information in some to ease the eye of the user. This approach looks less cluttered and allows the user to find what they are looking for on the page more easily. This is especially important for this page of the site specifically because users on the information page are likely searching for a specific piece of information and allowing them to find it while reading as little as possible is ideal to make the site easy to use and efficient for the user.

### Events Page:

In the original wireframes and iterations of my website I had the unordered list in the navigation bar have events second to last followed by nearby. Upon further consideration it became apparent that the events page will likely be used less by users than the nearby page. To make it easier for the user, I switched the two in the navigation bar so the user does not have to read as much to find the nearby page as more users will likely be looking for the nearby page rather than the events page. 

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXf1RhNT6eQdjKiBavkdei4Dscd6hzHE4a9BvwlqYHgAudbsfPGD2YNrYEWG3dX-87JGco0zmXB68OPQo73yGpB2jYLDSXtsSvhNl0p-6usP5lxjb0j2d42l1Kr_N5wg9gNHBUJdFwvzfJ18gliAfEqYhLv1?key=SUZYEnIdxil1W5SP97xvwg)

On the desktop version of the site there is a calendar that lays out the dates of upcoming events that the user can sign up for. This feature will help the user visualize the date of the event. On mobile, this feature makes the page more cluttered and for that reason I decided to hide the calendar on the mobile display in order to streamline the page in a smaller screen size.

### Gallery Page:

Following a critique of the design, I decided to remove the gallery page and just have a preview of the page on the home page instead. This allows users to briefly see the page but if they would like to see more they can click the see more button which directs them to the beach’s instagram page. An added bonus of this feature is that it allows the staff of the beach to more easily manage the content as they do not have to change and upload photos onto the site as often and can just use instagram.

### Nearby Page:

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXeSe-JgiXZRKkzogQ2WnD9rG-s2xZGeASYa2BTy-6CLUFzhUweJXQiCPrXsRrWtQFLKSzMigPQjrb6HPjXFQHylSwYOTDiw6ww8HSyf9j7O--sjvay-QEJiFpykP7lOKg_F-DjCFgMRW8NoOk7q8ij28H8?key=SUZYEnIdxil1W5SP97xvwg)

Initially, I had the title of the page as vertical text on the right side which was purely an aesthetics decision, however, this was less intuitive when I implemented it on the site and looked a bit confusing. As a result, I moved the title back to the top of the page in regular orientation to help the user understand the page more easily. 

I made the image that was originally just supposed to sit to the side of the text for each dropdown but having the image be the background of the box with the text overlaying it ended up being more aesthetically pleasing. I edited the photos to be dark enough to have significant contrast to the white text as well as adding a shadow on the text to further make the titles of the drop downs pop. 

![](https://lh7-rt.googleusercontent.com/docsz/AD_4nXfSaR0ZEJudSeQPrzxnSEqJLkiFqxO2kA36ywjbr9W0Dc4_AHHAlWlZ0mDG6AYAjtvh9o-kMNVX59jt93RUYZR-0o1DaAxuKx82ZVzuq_VDMs6tvZBhIw4nFcOV-0QS8sQ-AWXXRB9t2ZmHPzZglEwmhHAV?key=SUZYEnIdxil1W5SP97xvwg)

Within the dropdowns I removed the images for each store as the alignment would have been confusing had I made it right justified but it would have made the drop down much too long had I added it inline with the text on the left. Because the addresses are included, the user can find an image of the store if they are confused but the information did not feel necessary to include in the dropdown.

## Further Iterations and Improvements
For future iterations and improvements to the Bronte Beach website, consider integrating an interactive calendar to keep visitors informed about upcoming events and activities. Embedding an Instagram feed will enhance the site’s visual appeal and provide real-time updates and community engagement. Implementing real-time data for the live updates section, similar to Google Maps’ crowd-sourcing feature, can inform visitors about current beach conditions and crowd levels. Additionally, allowing local businesses to advertise on the nearby page will promote local commerce and provide valuable information to visitors looking for nearby amenities and services. These enhancements will create a more dynamic, informative, and user-friendly experience for all visitors.
